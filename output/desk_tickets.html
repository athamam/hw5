Reflection

I ran all three tickets on 2026-10-11 after resetting the database, starting at $3,400.00. Everything below is based on what's in the 101, 102, 103 and Cash tabs on this page, plus the audit trail (output/audit_trail.json, entries from index 188 on). The short version: the crew followed every Campus Customs rule, landed the cash at exactly $160.00, and never sent a specialist it didn't need. Where it was weak was efficiency and thinking one step ahead about money.

1. How I'd evaluate the agents on each ticket, and why

I graded each ticket on six things. Rules and money come first, because a wrong payment or wrong cash number is worse for CC than a slow run:

3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools, and why

Ticket 102 is the clearest case. "Check the lease, check cash, request the rent payment, draft a note to the landlord" is a straight line with no real decision in it. One CC ops agent with get_lease, get_cash_balance, request_payment, draft_customer_message and update_ticket_status would have done it in about 5 tool calls and maybe 4–5 model calls (my estimate). The team used 16 model calls and 37,922 tokens on the main run, because each hand-off (Boss → Facilities → Accounting → back to Boss → Customer Service) re-sends the shop rules, the role prompt and the ticket to a new agent.

Ticket 101 shows the same problem from a different angle. Accounting didn't trust Inventory's report and re-ran list_vendors, check_vendor_can_ship and get_pricing itself. When the second agent redoes the first agent's work anyway, splitting them only adds cost.

The close-out runs too. Those 3 runs (58,032 tokens together) were basically "read the approvals, write a follow-up, mark resolved". That could have been one deterministic step in the approve route, or one small agent.

Where the team still earns its keep at CC:

  • Permissions: only Accounting can request money and only Customer Service can write to customers. With one agent, one bad prompt could reach every tool. With the team, the blast radius is smaller.
  • Customer voice: Customer Service never told Tauhid about the unpaid invoice 501. Keeping the "talk to customers" job separate kept internal money stuff out of the drafts (102's draft #3 is the one slip).
  • The audit trail reads by role: I can see "Accounting created approval #1" instead of one agent doing 20 things.

So for 3 tickets this size, one agent with tools would honestly have been simpler and about 3× cheaper. The team pays off when CC has more tickets, more money at stake, and needs separation of duties.

5. Three new CC problems this team could NOT solve, and what it would need

A. The October cash crunch

Straight off the Cash tab: we have $160, the next rent is $2,400 due 2026-10-02 (lease 1's new next_due), and Bulldog Print Co will bill $272 for PO #1 + PO #2. The Yale AI Club order is worth $1,044, but cash only goes out in this app. There's no tool to invoice the club or record their payment, so the money never shows up.

Today the team can only file requests that pay will refuse. It can't fix the problem.

Would need:

  • A Sales/Receivables agent with create_customer_invoice, record_customer_payment (cash in, also human-approved), and a customers table.
  • A cash_forecast tool that adds up pending payments and open POs and upcoming rent against cash. That would have flagged ticket 103's PO.
  • Facilities with a draft_landlord_message tool to ask Elm City Properties for a payment plan.
B. The restock actually arrives on 9/5

When the 1 tee and 12 hoodies show up, nothing in the app changes. There's no tool to receive a PO, so inventory stays at S = 0 and M = 8 forever. There's also no way to set aside 20 hoodies for the Yale AI Club or ship Tauhid his tee (QuickShip CT is in vendors as a courier, but there's no tool to book it).

Would need:

  • A Receiving/Fulfillment agent with receive_purchase_order (adds qty to inventory, marks the PO received, creates the vendor invoice), reserve_stock / fulfill_order (subtracts qty), and book_courier for QuickShip CT.

C. Actually talking to people, and time moving
